Hello!

I am a masters student at McMaster University in Hamilton, Canada. My areas of research include exoplanets, mantle–atmosphere coupling, and the origins of life. I am supervised by Dr. Ralph Pudritz.

My research experience ranges from Bayesian analysis of exoplanet transits to paleontology field work in the badlands of Dinosaur Provincial Park, Alberta. Currently, I am performing coupled simulations evolving the atmospheres and interiors of rocky planets, with the ultimate goal of determining what kinds of planets can produce life.

I am particularly interested in the way mantle chemistry drives the production of atmospheric methane and HCN, which subsequently react to form the building blocks of life.

I am an author and editor for Astrobites, a science communicator at the William J. Macallion Planetarium, a retired varsity rower, and a former improv club president who still likes to make people laugh 🙂
